**Floe sets no size limit on a direct transfer.** It does not cap one, because the file never
touches anything Floe pays for. Most transfers are [direct](/glossary#direct-connection).

**A relayed transfer is capped at 2 GB per session.** When a network blocks the direct path, the
file goes through a [relay](/glossary#relay) instead, and relay bandwidth costs real money. The cap
is what keeps Floe free.

So the practical answer to "how big a file can I send" is: as big as the receiving side can hold,
as long as the two devices can reach each other directly. See
[Direct connections](/how-it-works/direct-connection) for what decides that, and
[the two limits that are not Floe's](#two-other-limits-which-are-not-floes) for what the receiving
side can hold.

## How to stay on a direct connection {#how-to-stay-on-the-unlimited-path}

These improve your odds of a direct connection:

- Use a normal home or personal Wi-Fi network.
- When the two devices are in the same place, put both on the same Wi-Fi network.
- Disconnect from any VPN.
- Avoid strict corporate and university networks.
- Try a personal mobile hotspot when you cannot avoid one.

Two devices on ordinary home networks usually connect directly, even on different networks. A phone
on mobile data usually connects directly too. Some carriers put many customers behind one shared
address, called carrier-grade [NAT](/glossary#nat), and there a transfer can end up relayed. The
[badge](/glossary#badge) shows which route you got.

## What counts as one session {#what-counts-as-one-session}

A session is one sender and one receiver. The [room](/glossary#room) holds exactly two people, so
only one recipient is ever connected at a time. Each connection is checked on its own, and earlier
transfers do not count toward the 2 GB. With a [request link](/glossary#request-link), each drop is
its own session.

The sending app checks **once, before any file data moves**. When the connection turns out to be
relayed, it compares the total size of everything you queued against 2 GB and either allows the
whole transfer or refuses it. It is not a meter that stops you partway, so a 3 GB batch over a
relay is declined up front rather than truncated at 2 GB. A request link adds a second check on
the receiving side, which can stop a drop partway: see
[How the cap is enforced](#how-the-cap-is-enforced).

Exactly 2 GB is allowed. Only more than that is refused.

Starting a new transfer runs the check again from scratch, so splitting a large batch into pieces
under the cap works. Each drop through a request link is checked on its own the same way.
Deliberately cycling sessions to get around the cap goes against the spirit of a free service.

## What you see when it stops you {#what-you-see-when-it-stops-you}

**In the browser**, nothing is sent, and the sending page shows three things:

- The status line
  [`Transfer blocked. Relay limit exceeded.`](/troubleshooting#transfer-blocked-relay-limit-exceeded)
- An amber notice: "Transfer limit exceeded. Relay connections are capped at 2 GB. Reload the page
  to send 2 GB or less, or to send from a network that supports a direct connection."
- The cap after the total in the file count line, for example "3 · 2.5 GB / 2 GB".

**In the [CLI](/glossary#cli)**, the send fails with the line below, where the figure in
parentheses is the total you selected:

```text Output
Error: transfer blocked: relay connections are capped at 2 GB (selected 2.5 GB)
```

**In Floe Desktop**, the status line shows the same sentence, and adds
`Turn off Hide my IP to send larger files` when Hide my IP is on.

**On a request link's page**, the sender sees a card titled "Relay limit" and nothing is sent.
When the link maker's Floe Desktop stops the drop partway instead, the page reads "Relayed drops
are capped at 2 GB", and the files saved before that stay on their computer. See
[Sending through a request link](/web-app/request-links#when-their-floe-stops-the-drop).

In every case the sending app tells the receiving side why the transfer stopped.

## One case that catches people out {#one-case-that-catches-people-out}

Floe Desktop's [Hide my IP address](/desktop/settings#hide-my-ip-address) setting, and the CLI's
`--relay-only` flag, route every transfer through the relay on purpose, so the other person never
sees your [IP address](/glossary#ip-address). While either is on, **the 2 GB cap applies to every
transfer**, even on a network that could easily have connected directly. Turn it off, or drop the
flag, to send more. On a request link's page, the **Hide my IP (relay only, 2 GB per drop)**
checkbox does the same to that drop. The glossary lists every name
[Hide my IP](/glossary#hide-my-ip) goes by.

You never need to turn relay fallback off to send something large. The cap applies only when the
connection actually ends up relayed, so leaving fallback on costs a direct transfer nothing and
saves the transfer that would otherwise have failed.

## Two other limits, which are not Floe's {#two-other-limits-which-are-not-floes}

A **browser receiver** keeps what it receives inside the browser until you leave the page, so how
much it can take depends on the browser and on the size of the whole transfer. A phone holds less
than a computer, and how much depends on the phone. See
[Known limitations](/how-it-works/known-limitations#a-browser-receiver-is-limited-by-what-the-browser-can-hold)
for what each browser can hold, and what to do when the receiver is a phone.

Floe Desktop and the CLI write straight to disk as bytes arrive and have no such ceiling, which
makes them the better choice for anything multi-gigabyte.

Your **disk** is the other one. Nothing checks that the receiving device has room before the
transfer starts, except for a request link. There, Floe Desktop checks before each file that the
drive will keep 2 GB free after it, and stops the drop if it will not.

Every other limit and timeout, with its number, is in [Limits](/reference/limits).

## How the cap is enforced {#how-the-cap-is-enforced}

The cap is 2 × 1024 × 1024 × 1024 bytes (2,147,483,648), which Floe's messages write as 2 GB.
It is local policy on the sending side, applied identically by the web app, the desktop app, and
the CLI. It is not part of the wire protocol, and an ordinary receiver runs no relay check of
its own.

A request link is the exception. The Floe Desktop that receives the drop also reads the route
itself when the drop is accepted. If the route is relayed, it refuses any file that would take the
drop past 2 GB. The files saved before that one stay saved, so this check can stop a drop partway.
It catches a relayed drop that got past the sender's own check.

The sender inspects the network path that actually won once the connection is established. A
direct connection always passes. If the route cannot be determined, the sender allows the
transfer rather than blocking a legitimate one. That is deliberate: a probe hiccup should never
stop a transfer that would have worked. The consequence is that a relayed transfer over 2 GB goes
ahead in the rare case where Floe could not tell it was relayed.

For self-hosters: the limit lives in `RELAY_SIZE_LIMIT` in `client/lib/relay.ts` and
`RelaySizeLimit` in `cli/engine/transfer/relay.go`, which the desktop app also reads for that
receiving-side check. It is compiled into all three clients rather than served by the
[signaling server](/glossary#signaling-server), so raising it on a
[self-hosted](/glossary#self-hosted) instance means building the clients from modified source. See
[TURN relay](/self-hosting/turn-relay), which sets up the [TURN](/glossary#turn) relay itself.
