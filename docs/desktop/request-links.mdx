A request link lets one person send files to your PC from their browser, with nothing to install on their
side. You make the link in Floe Desktop and send it to them. When they click **Send**, your Floe asks you
first, and nothing is saved until you click **Accept**, unless you made the link with
[Auto-accept](#auto-accept) on. The files then stream straight to your PC and are never stored on a Floe
server.

Request links are in Beta, in Floe Desktop for Windows.

**Someone sent you a request link?** See [Sending through a request link](/web-app/request-links).

## What a request link is {#what-a-request-link-is}

- **One person, one drop.** A drop is one sending through the link. While one person is connected, nobody
  else can use the link, and after a drop finishes or stops the link is used up.
- **A live transfer, not a mailbox.** Files move only while Floe is open on your PC and the PC is awake and
  online. Nothing waits on a server for you to collect later.
- **No names attached.** Floe has no accounts, so a link carries no name. The sender's page says "Floe does
  not know who that is", and it cannot tell you who is sending either.
- **One at a time.** You can have one request link open. Close it to make another.

## Where to find it {#where-to-find-it}

In Floe Desktop, open **Receive**. **Request link** sits next to **Code**, with a small **Beta** tag.
There is no switch to turn on, and nothing about request links is sent until you click **Make link**.

Request links need a server that offers them. Floe's own server does unless they have been turned off
there. When the server does not offer them, **Make link** says so under the button: "Request links are off
on this server". If Floe cannot reach the server at all, it says "Couldn't make a link". For your own
server, see [Self-hosted servers](#self-hosted-servers).

## Make a link {#make-a-link}

Go to **Receive** and choose **Request link**. The form has three fields and a check:

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Label** | Optional, up to 64 characters. Only you see it: it names the request when it arrives, the folder the files go into, and the History row. It never leaves your PC. |
| **Save to** | The folder every drop goes into. Leave it blank for `Downloads\Floe`, which the field shows in gray. |
| **Link ends** | **In 30 minutes**, **In 1 hour**, **In 8 hours**, **In 24 hours** (the default), **In 3 days** or **In 7 days**. |
| **Auto-accept** | A check under **Link ends**, off on every new link and never remembered. With it on, drops through this link save without asking, except unusual ones. See [Auto-accept](#auto-accept). |

In **Save to**, a relative path such as `Pictures\Drops` is read from your home folder, so that one is the
Drops folder in your Pictures folder. Variables such as `%USERPROFILE%` and a leading `~` work, and the
quotes that File Explorer's **Copy as path** adds are dropped. Floe checks the folder when you click **Make
link**. If it cannot use it, because its drive is not there, a network share is offline, part of the path is
a file, a name in it has a character Windows does not allow, such as `|` or `?`, a name ends in a space
or in a period after a space or another period, or it is a path Floe cannot place, such as `\Floe` or
`D:Floe`, it says "Couldn't use that folder" under the button and makes no link. A **Save to** of
only quotes or spaces means the default folder.

Click **Make link**. Above the button, Floe says who can see your IP address: "Senders see your IP, even if
you decline". With Hide my IP on, it says "Hide my IP limits drops to 2 GB" instead. While **Auto-accept**
is checked, an amber line under it reads "Anyone with this link can send you files without asking".

### Who sees your IP address {#who-sees-your-ip-address}

The sender's browser and your PC connect to each other directly, and a direct connection shows each side the
other's IP address. That happens as soon as the sender clicks **Send**, which is before your Floe asks you to
accept or decline, so declining does not hide your address.

Turn on **Hide my IP** and Floe relays the drop instead, so the sender sees the relay's address and not yours.
A relayed drop is capped at 2 GB. Floe reads the setting when a sender connects, so a change applies to the
next sender. See [Hide my IP address](/desktop/settings#hide-my-ip-address) and
[What each side can see](#what-each-side-can-see).

### Auto-accept {#auto-accept}

Check **Auto-accept** under **Link ends** before you click **Make link**, and drops through that one link
save without the Accept step. It is off on every new link, and Floe never remembers it. Its info button
warns "Only turn this on if you trust everyone with the link", then says "Files save without asking, except
in a few cases, like low space or a USB drive". Anyone with the whole link can use it, a link can be
forwarded, and the person sending cannot tell that the link accepts by itself.

While such a link waits, its line reads "Ends today, 2:05 PM · Accepts automatically" and the status at the
top reads **AUTO-ACCEPT**. A drop that qualifies starts at once. With notifications on and Floe not in front,
Windows shows "Receiving files through your request link", with no taskbar flash and no `(1) Floe` title, the card shows the drop arriving, **Cancel
drop** stops it as usual, and History marks it "Accepted automatically". If two automatic Accepts on one
link find the sender already gone within 10 minutes, Floe stops showing that notification for the link.

Some drops still ask, with **Accept** and **Decline** as usual:

- A drop the request would warn about: too little free space, a file too large for the drive, or over 2 GB
  through the relay.
- A drop whose route Floe could not read.
- A drop to a drive that cannot carry Windows' downloaded-file mark, such as FAT32 and exFAT drives and
  many network shares, or to a drive that does not answer in time.
- A drop that would leave less than 20 GB, or less than a tenth of the drive, free, and a drop to a drive
  whose free space or size Floe could not read.

When a drop asks for one of the last two reasons, the request says why in one gray line: "Asks because C:
would have under 48.0 GB free", "Asks because Windows can't mark files on G:", or "Asks because Floe couldn't
check D:". Floe also reads the drive when you make the link and every 10 minutes while it waits. When the
drive already has less free space than an automatic drop must leave, every drop would ask, so the line under
the link reads "Ends today, 2:05 PM · Asks first, low space on C:" instead.

Everything else about a drop stays the same: one drop per link, at most 10,000 files, 2 GB kept free after
each file, renamed shortcut-type files, and the sender sees your IP address unless Hide my IP is on.

## Share the link {#share-the-link}

Floe shows the link, **Copy link**, and a line such as "Ends today, 2:05 PM". A later day reads like "Ends
Oct 9, 2:05 PM", and a link made with Auto-accept adds " · Accepts automatically". A request link looks like
`https://floe.one/r/<link id>#<key>`.

Send it to one person, through a channel you trust. The whole link matters, including everything after the
`#`: that part is the key that connects their browser to your PC. Browsers do not send it when they load a
page, so the web server behind floe.one never sees it. The sender's page uses it only when they click
**Send**, to reach your PC through Floe's signaling server. Anyone who has the whole link can use it, and a
link can be forwarded.

While the link waits, Floe reads "Waiting for files". Floe cannot tell when the link is opened, so a chat
app's preview of it changes nothing. Floe hears from the sender only when they click **Send**.

**Close link** ends the link at any time.

### The status at the top {#the-status-at-the-top}

The status at the top right reads **READY** while a link waits, the same as when no link is open, or
**AUTO-ACCEPT** while a link made with Auto-accept waits. Open **Receive** > **Request link** to see your
link. The dot beside it is green, or amber when Hide my IP is on, because then whoever uses the link next is
relayed and capped at 2 GB.

While files arrive it reads **ACTIVE** until the route is known, then **DIRECT** or **RELAY**, like any
transfer. A relayed drop is capped at 2 GB.
See [Sending files](/desktop/sending#while-it-sends) for every word the status can show.

### A request link pasted into Receive {#a-request-link-pasted-into-receive}

A request link is for sending, so it does not work in the **Code or link** field. Floe says "Request links
open in a web browser" and offers **Open in browser**. The `floe` CLI refuses it too, in its own words:
"That is a request link for sending files to someone. Open it in a web browser."

## Sending through a request link {#sending-through-a-request-link}

If someone sent you a request link, you are the sender, and your guide is
[Sending through a request link](/web-app/request-links). It covers picking files, waiting for the other
person to accept, keeping the page open, and reporting a link.

## Accept or decline {#accept-or-decline}

When the sender clicks **Send**, Floe connects to their computer and then shows the request under
**Receive** > **Request link**. On a link made with Auto-accept, only a drop that still asks shows one (see
[Auto-accept](#auto-accept)). If you are somewhere else in Floe, a notice with **Review** appears, the
taskbar button flashes, and the window title reads `(1) Floe`. If notifications are on and Floe is in the
background, Windows also shows a notification. See [Notifications](/desktop/settings#notifications).

The request shows four things:

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Your label | The label you gave the link, in the heading, such as **ACME FOOTAGE WANTS TO SEND YOU FILES**. It is your own label, not the sender's name. With no label it reads **SOMEONE WANTS TO SEND YOU FILES**. |
| How much | The number of files and their total size, as the sender's browser announced them. |
| Where it saves | **Into**, then the new folder the files will go into. |
| How long you have | **Accept** counts down the time left, in minutes and seconds, such as **Accept (9:45)**. |

It never shows a file name or any other text the sender chose. Names appear only as files arrive, cleaned of
hidden characters. Floe holds the sender to the count and size you accepted: more data stops the drop.

The request also warns you, in amber, when something will stop the drop: too little free space, a drive that
cannot hold a file this large, or a drop over 2 GB through the relay. On a link made with Auto-accept, a
drop that asks for a reason those lines do not cover, such as the free space an automatic drop must leave,
says why in a gray line (see [Auto-accept](#auto-accept)). A laptop needs one more precaution: see
[On a laptop](#on-a-laptop).

<Warning>
  **Accept only if you expect files from the person you sent this link to.** A link can be forwarded, and
  Floe cannot tell who is on the other end, so decline anything you do not expect.
</Warning>

**Accept** and **Decline** wake up one second after the request appears, and again for a second whenever the
Floe window comes to the front, so a click meant for something else cannot answer it.

You have just under 10 minutes to answer, and **Accept** counts it down. After that the request ends on its
own and the sender's page says they did not answer in time. A missed request does not close the link: it
waits again, with a note of the time you missed.

If the sender's connection fails while the request is showing, because their browser closed or crashed or
their network went away, the request ends on its own about 30 seconds later and the link waits again. A drop
whose sender's connection fails stops the same way, and the files already saved stay.

After **Decline**, nothing is saved: Floe shows "Request declined" and asks "Keep waiting for the right
sender?". **Keep waiting** opens the link again for them; **Close link** ends it. Until you choose, the link
takes no new request. If two requests end without an Accept within 10 minutes, Floe stops showing Windows
notifications for the next ones on that link, though the flash, the title and the notice stay, and asks
whether to close it.

## Where files go {#where-files-go}

Each drop gets its own new folder inside your **Save to** folder, created only when the drop is accepted, by
you or by Auto-accept. It is named from your label and this PC's clock, such as `Acme footage 2026-09-14
1405`, or `Request 2026-09-14 1405` with no label. The time is on a 24-hour clock, so the folders sort by
date. If the name is taken, Floe adds a number, such as `(2)`, and never writes into a folder that already
exists.

Inside it, the sender's folders keep their structure. A file shows as `name.ext.part` while it arrives and
takes its real name only once it is complete. Names Windows cannot use are adjusted, as for any receive (see
[Receiving files](/desktop/receiving#names-windows-cannot-represent-are-adjusted)).

## While files arrive {#while-files-arrive}

The card shows which file is arriving, how much has arrived, the speed, and the time left. **Cancel drop** stops
it. You can minimize the window. From the moment the drop is accepted until it ends, Floe asks Windows to keep
the PC awake; the screen can still turn off. It does not keep the PC awake while a link only waits.

There is no resume. If the connection drops, the drop stops: files already saved stay, the file that was
moving is discarded, and the sender can send the rest with a new link.

A single drop stops at 24 hours. When a drop would take longer, the card says so. For a drop that will take
hours, keep the PC plugged in and awake, and set Windows Update's active hours to cover it, because Windows
may restart for updates outside your active hours.

### On a laptop {#on-a-laptop}

Keep a laptop plugged in with its lid open while files arrive. A laptop on battery can stop Floe a few minutes
after its screen turns off, and closing the lid does the same. The drop then stops, the files already saved
stay, and the file that was moving has to be sent again through a new link.

On a PC with a battery, the card reminds you with "Keep this laptop plugged in and open" under the progress.
It appears once the drop has run for a minute with more than 5 minutes left, and stays until that drop ends,
so a short drop never shows it. A PC without a battery never shows it.

## When it finishes {#when-it-finishes}

The card reads, for example, **RECEIVED 12 FILES, 38.0 GB**, followed by a green check mark when every file
was checked (see [The check mark](#the-sha-256-check)). If notifications are on and Floe is in the
background, Windows shows a notification. Under the heading the card lists every file that arrived, each
with its size, in a box that scrolls after about five rows ("+ 3 more" counts any beyond the 200 names it
keeps), with the drop's folder and **Show in folder** at the bottom of the box. **Make another link** puts
the result away and takes you back to the form for a new link. The link you used is used up.

History keeps one row per drop. See [History](#history).

### The check mark {#the-sha-256-check}

A green check mark after the heading means every file's SHA-256 matched: the value was computed on both
computers and the two were equal. A screen reader says "SHA-256 matched". Floe never shows the value itself.

No check mark means Floe could not check every file, usually because the sender's page or app is older and sent
no SHA-256 for some file, or because the sender's browser had not finished computing one when it stopped
waiting, at most 45 seconds after the file's last byte, and sent the file without it. The files are still
saved. A file that does not match is never kept: it is deleted, the drop stops, and the card says so. See
[If a drop stops](#if-a-drop-stops).

Your Floe computes the SHA-256 while writing the file, before the file takes its real name, and never reads the
file back from disk. The check proves the file arrived as the sender's browser read it. It says nothing about
whether the file is harmful.

The same check mark shows in History for a drop, and after a send or a receive with a code, when every file
was checked.

### Renamed files {#renamed-files}

Some Windows file types are opened by File Explorer as soon as it shows a folder, and a few of them can send
your Windows sign-in to a server the sender chose. A drop saves these with `.floe-blocked` added to the name,
so File Explorer leaves them alone: `.lnk`, `.url`, `.website`, `.library-ms`, `.searchConnector-ms`,
`.search-ms`, `.scf`, `.theme`, `.themepack`, `.deskthemepack`, and any file called `desktop.ini`. A folder
or file name that ends in a class ID, such as `.{00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000}`, loses that
ending.

The card says how many files were renamed, and **Show in folder** asks first: "This drop has renamed files"
and "Open the folder anyway?". Rename a file back only if you trust it.

### Floe does not scan files {#floe-does-not-scan-files}

Floe does not scan received files for malware. It marks each file as downloaded, the way a browser does, so
SmartScreen and Office Protected View treat it like any download. The mark needs a drive that keeps it, such
as NTFS; FAT32 and exFAT drives, like most USB sticks and SD cards, and many network shares do not, and a
file saved there arrives without it. See
[Received files are marked as downloads](/desktop/receiving#received-files-are-marked-as-downloads).

The check mark means the files arrived intact. It does not mean they are safe.

The finished card does not repeat this on every drop. Floe shows "Floe doesn't scan files for malware" there
only when your save drive cannot carry the downloaded-file mark, such as a USB stick, an SD card, or many
network shares, and when Floe could not find out what the drive can carry. On those drives the files carry no
downloaded-file mark, so Windows has nothing to warn you from when you open one, and the line takes the place
of that warning. On a drive that keeps the mark, Windows applies its usual caution to downloads (SmartScreen
for programs, Protected View for Office documents), so the card says nothing about it.

## If a drop stops {#if-a-drop-stops}

The card reads **DROP STOPPED** with one sentence saying why and how many files were saved. Files saved
before the stop stay in the drop's folder, and **Show in folder** opens it when there is one. The file that
was arriving is deleted, with one exception: when Windows will not let Floe move a finished file into place
(often because another program is holding it), Floe retries for 5 minutes, and if it still fails, the
complete file stays in the drop's folder with a `.part` ending.

After a stop, the sender can send the rest with a new link: click **Make another link** and send them that.
Every stop sentence is listed under [Messages](#messages-on-your-pc).

With **Contribute to global stats** on, the files a drop saved before it stopped count toward Floe's public
total, as a finished drop's do. See [Contribute to global stats](/desktop/settings#contribute-to-global-stats).

## How a link ends {#how-a-link-ends}

| How | What you see |
|---|---|
| Its end time passes | "Link ended at 2:05 PM". A request or a drop that began before then keeps going. |
| You click **Close link** | "Link closed" |
| A drop finishes or stops | The result card; the link is used up. |
| You close Floe | Floe asks first ("Your request link stops working", with **Keep open** and **Close**). If you reopen Floe before the link's end time, **Receive** opens on **Request link**, ready for a new link. |
| Request links are turned off on the server | "Request links are off on this server" |

If Floe loses its connection to the Floe server, the card reads "Can't reach the Floe server" and
"Reconnecting...", with **Retry now**. Floe keeps trying until the link's end time, and a server restart does
not end the link: it comes back when Floe reconnects. Meanwhile the sender's page says your computer is not
connected.

**Start over** on the Send side leaves the link open. So does switching tabs or opening Settings.

## What each side can see {#what-each-side-can-see}

<RequestLinkVisibility />

The sender's browser and your PC exchange IP addresses as soon as the sender connects, before you accept, so
declining does not hide yours. Turn on Hide my IP (see
[Hide my IP address](/desktop/settings#hide-my-ip-address)) to show the relay's address instead. Floe reads
the setting when a sender connects, so a change applies to the next sender.

Hide my IP sends every drop through the relay, which caps a drop at 2 GB. The sender's page has its own Hide
my IP with the same cost.

Floe's server introduces the two computers, as it does for any transfer, and never receives the files. See
[Security and privacy](/security-privacy).

## Limits {#limits}

<RequestLinkLimits />

FAT and FAT32 drives cannot hold a file of 4 GB or more; exFAT, NTFS and ReFS drives can. On such a drive the
request warns you when the first file is too large, and any file that is too large stops the drop there.

A relayed drop over 2 GB stops before any file is saved. The sender's page checks first and says "Relay
limit" when the connection needs the relay. See [File size limits](/how-it-works/2gb-limit).

| "The sender couldn't connect" | Someone clicked **Send**, but their browser and your PC could not finish connecting. The link waits again. | They can try again. If it keeps happening, see [The transfer never starts](/troubleshooting#the-transfer-never-starts). |
| "2 requests ended without Accept in 10 minutes. Close this link?" | Two requests on this link ended without an Accept, so Floe stopped showing Windows notifications for the next ones. The flash, the title and the notice stay. | Close the link if you expect nothing more through it. |

| "Asks because Floe couldn't check D:" | On a link made with Auto-accept, Floe could not read the save drive's free space or size, or the drive did not say in time whether it can carry the downloaded-file mark, so the drop asks. | Accept or decline as usual. |
| "Request links open in a web browser" | Under **Receive**, when a request link is pasted into **Code or link**. | Click **Open in browser**. |

When a drop stops, the card reads **DROP STOPPED** with one of these, and **Make another link** under it:

| Card | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "The drive ran out of space · 4 of 12 files saved" | The save drive filled up. | Free some space or choose another folder, then send the sender a new link for the rest. |
| "A file didn't match what was sent and was deleted" | A file's SHA-256 on your PC did not match the sender's. | Send a new link; they can send the rest again. |
| "A folder path was too long for Windows" | A file's path in the drop was too deep or too long to save. | Ask the sender to zip deeply nested folders first. |
| "More data arrived than you accepted" | The sender's browser sent more than the count and size you accepted, often because files changed after they chose them. | Send a new link; they choose the files again. |
| "Over the 2 GB relay limit · Nothing saved" | The drop went through the relay and would have gone past 2 GB. | Turn off Hide my IP, or ask for under 2 GB per drop. |
| "A file is too large for this drive" | The save drive cannot hold a file that large, such as 4 GB or more on FAT32. | Save to an NTFS or exFAT drive. |
| "Windows couldn't write to the folder" | Windows refused a write into the drop's folder. | Check the save folder, then send a new link. |
| "Windows blocked Floe from saving a file" | Floe could not move a finished file into place, often because another program held it. Floe retried for 5 minutes first. | A second line says "The complete file was kept with a .part ending". The file is complete: remove the `.part` ending to use it. |
| "You stopped this drop · 4 of 12 files saved" | You clicked **Cancel drop**. | Nothing, or send a new link for the rest. |
| "The sender stopped this drop · 4 of 12 files saved" | The sender stopped sending. | Nothing, unless they want to send the rest. |
| "The drop reached the 24-hour limit · 4 of 12 files saved" | A single drop stops at 24 hours. | Send a new link for the rest. |
| "4 of 12 files saved" | The drop stopped for another reason, such as a lost connection. | Send a new link for the rest. |

A card without a count of its own is followed by one after a middle dot, for example "· 4 of 12 files
saved". With a single file it reads "1 of 1 file saved", and with none "Nothing saved", except beside a kept
`.part` file.

## Self-hosted servers {#self-hosted-servers}

A self-hosted server offers request links only when its policy file turns them on, and until then **Make
link** says "Request links are off on this server". See [Turn on request links](/self-hosting/request-links).

## History {#history}

Each drop that finishes, or stops after saving a file, adds one row to
[History](/desktop/receiving#history), titled with your label if you gave one. Opening it shows the check
mark when every file was checked, "Accepted automatically" for a drop that Auto-accept took, and why it
stopped. It repeats no renamed-files warning (that stays on the finished card), but **Show in folder** still
asks first after renames. Declined and missed requests add nothing.
