Someone sent you a Floe link, or a QR code. Open it in any modern browser and the files arrive on
their own. There is nothing to install, no account, and nothing to click to start.

<Info>
A link with `/r/` in it is a [request link](/glossary#request-link). It is for sending files to the
person who made it, not for receiving: see [Sending through a request link](/web-app/request-links).
</Info>

## The short version {#the-short-version}

Open the link and keep the tab open. The files arrive by themselves, each with its own download
button. Your browser holds them until you save them, so save every file before you close the tab.
With two or more files, **Download ZIP** saves them all in one archive, folders included.

## Open the link

Click the link, or point your phone's camera at the QR code. The page joins the sender's
[room](/glossary#room), the meeting point for this one transfer, and connects to the sender by
itself. A link made in the web app works while the sender's page stays open and connected to
Floe's [server](/glossary#signaling-server), which introduces the two devices. See
[How long a code and a link last](/how-it-works/signaling#code-and-link-lifetime).

An older Floe link that carries `?room=` in place of `#room=` still opens the same way.

### If Floe says "Open in your browser" {#if-floe-says-open-in-your-browser}

Apps like Facebook, Messenger, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, LINE, X, and WeChat open links in
their own [in-app browser](/glossary#in-app-browser) rather than in Chrome or Safari. Those built-in
browsers handle file transfers poorly, so Floe recognizes them and shows this screen instead of the
transfer view. It does the same when floe.one itself is opened in one of those apps to send.

The screen names the app (it calls X "Twitter"), or says "This in-app browser" for some other
Android apps. Any other app's built-in browser is not recognized, and the transfer view opens in it
directly.

<Frame caption="What Floe shows when a link is opened inside another app's browser">
  <img src="/images/web/06-in-app-browser.png" style={{ width: "100%", maxWidth: "320px", borderRadius: "12px" }} alt="The Open in your browser screen, with two steps to reach Safari, the page link, a Copy link to paste in browser button and a Continue anyway link." />
</Frame>

Follow the two steps on the screen:

- **On Android:** tap the ⋮ menu at the top right, then "Open in browser" or "Open in Chrome".
- **On an iPhone, an iPad or anything else:** tap the icon of an arrow leaving a box, or the ···
  menu, then "Open in Safari" or "Open in Browser".

Or tap **Copy link to paste in browser** and open the link yourself in Chrome, Safari, Edge, or
Firefox. The button reads "Link copied" for 2 seconds when the copy worked. The screen also shows
the link itself. If the app blocks copying, the screen says "Couldn’t copy. Press and hold the link
above to copy it." **Continue anyway (some features may not work)** opens the transfer view there
regardless.

Until you tap **Continue anyway**, the page has not joined the transfer. Opening the link inside the
app therefore does not use it up, and you can still open it in your own browser.

## While you wait

The card walks through three steps as it gets ready: "Secure room joined", "Waiting for the sender
to start" and "Files stream in below". It also offers one choice.

<Frame caption="The receiving card before the first file arrives">
  <img src="/images/web/04-receive-waiting.png" style={{ width: "100%", maxWidth: "520px", borderRadius: "12px" }} alt="The Floe receive card with a green Direct badge, Secure room joined, Waiting for the sender to start, and a checked Contribute to global stats box." />
</Frame>

The [badge](/glossary#badge) at the top right means what it does on the sending side: see
[Watch the connection](/web-app/sending#watch-the-connection). Two things differ on this page.
There is no ping beside it. And it turns **Offline** once the connection to the sender has closed,
which is how a finished transfer from `floe send` or Floe Desktop ends.

The transfer starts by itself, usually a couple of seconds after the connection is established.
A sender in the browser checks the route first. When it goes through a [relay](/glossary#relay) and
they turned relay fallback off or queued more than 2 GB, their page stops before any file moves.
Your page then shows a red banner with their reason. Ask them to do what it says, then open the new link they send.

### Contribute to global stats

Floe's homepage carries a public, all-time count of bytes moved by everyone, shown under the card
as an amount "transferred globally". When the last file of a transfer lands, the receiving side
adds this transfer's size to that total, and nothing else. No file names, no contents, nothing
identifying, and the report never slows or blocks the transfer. A transfer that stops before its
last file reports nothing, and neither does one made only of empty files.

It is on by default. The checkbox shows from the moment the page opens and disappears once the first
file lands, so decide while you are waiting. Your choice is kept in this browser, so unchecking it
once covers your later transfers too.

<StatsOptOut />

The total is only ever shown on the homepage. It is read from the server every 10 seconds, and
your own transfer is added to it at once. See
[Security and privacy](/security-privacy#aggregate-statistics) for the full data-handling note.

## Saving what arrives

Every file gets its own download button the moment it lands, so you can start saving while the
rest are still coming. A saved file goes where your browser puts every download, usually the
Downloads folder. On an iPhone or iPad, Safari puts downloads in the Files app.

<Frame caption="A finished transfer, with per-file buttons and the two bulk options">
  <img src="/images/web/05-receive-downloads.png" style={{ width: "100%", maxWidth: "520px", borderRadius: "12px" }} alt="The Floe receive card with Download All and Download ZIP above three received files, and a green check mark before 3 files received." />
</Frame>

While a file arrives, a progress row reads, for example, "Receiving file 2 of 3", then the speed,
the time left and a percentage. The percentage is for the current file only, so it starts again at
0 for each file. Once a file's bytes are all in, the row reads "Verifying file 2 of 3" while Floe
checks it.

Between files, the line under the list reads "Received 1 of 3 files. Waiting for the rest". The
check mark and a count such as "3 files received" appear only once every file the sender
announced has arrived. The check mark is green only when every file's SHA-256 matched, as
described further down. A smaller line under the list, such as "3 files (8.02 MB)", counts what has
arrived so far.

The list becomes a scroll box once it holds more than four files, and it scrolls to the newest file
by itself. Hover over a name to see it in full.

On a transfer of two or more files, **Download All** and **Download ZIP** appear as soon as the
second file lands. They hide again while the next file is arriving or being checked and come back
between files, so if they vanish mid-transfer nothing is wrong. Once the transfer finishes they
stay put.

- **Download All** saves each file separately, about one every 300 ms. Your browser may ask you to
  allow multiple downloads.
- **Download ZIP** bundles everything into one archive named `floe_transfer_<timestamp>.zip`. The
  number in the name is the time the archive was finished, in milliseconds, not the time you
  clicked. Files go into it as they are, without compression, so the archive is about as large
  as the files together. An archive of 4 GB or more, or of more than 65,534 files, is refused
  before any file is read: see
  [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting#could-not-prepare-files-for-zip-or-zip-creation-failed).

While either one runs, both buttons are grayed out, and the one you clicked reads "Downloading..."
or "Zipping...". A small card shows each step with a count and a percentage. For **Download All**
it reads "Downloading: report.pdf" for each file. For **Download ZIP** it reads
"Processing: report.pdf" while that file is written into the archive, a piece at a time, then
"Creating ZIP archive..." while the archive is finished. A large file can sit on its line for a
while. It cannot be canceled.

The two buttons differ in more than convenience. A ZIP keeps any folder structure the sender used
and numbers duplicate names inside the archive. Saving files one at a time flattens the structure
and leaves duplicate names to your browser.

A single-file transfer shows only that file's own button, and no bulk options. That also means a
folder that held one file arrives without its folder.

**On iOS, use Download ZIP** when there is more than one file. Saving them individually behaves
inconsistently in Safari and in iOS web views. The exception is a very large transfer: on iOS
the archive is held in memory beside the files, so if zipping fails, save them from their own
rows instead.

The names in the list are made safe to save, so they can differ from what the sender sees:

- Only the file's own name shows, without its folders. Two files of the same name from different
  folders look the same in the list.
- Characters that Windows does not allow in a name, such as `:` and `?`, become `_`.
- A name with nothing usable in it is saved as `received_file`.

A file that fails a check never reaches the list. Floe discards a file whose size does not match
what the sender announced, or whose [SHA-256](/glossary#sha-256), a fingerprint of its contents,
does not match when the sender provides one. The rest of the transfer stops with it. When every
file arrived and every SHA-256 matched, the line "3 files received" under the list, with your own
number, gets a green check mark (the [SHA-256 matched](/glossary#sha-256-matched) mark). When some
file could be checked by its size only, the check mark is gray. See
[File checks](/how-it-works/file-checks).

Floe does not scan files for malware. A file that passes its checks arrived intact, which does not
make it safe to open and says nothing about who sent it. See
[Known limitations](/how-it-works/known-limitations#floe-does-not-scan-received-files).

<Warning>
Files stay with the browser until you save them, so closing or reloading the tab discards anything
you have not saved. Opening a different Floe link in the same tab reloads it too. One case is
different: a link from `floe` or Floe Desktop opened in a tab that holds another such link. While a
receive is under way or a file that arrived is unsaved, your browser asks about leaving if you have
clicked on the page. If you have not, the page opens the new link only once that is over.
</Warning>

While a receive is under way, or a file that arrived has not been saved yet, your browser asks
before the tab is closed or reloaded, or Back leaves the page. It also asks before the Floe button
or one of the page's own links takes you away. A file counts as saved once its own download button,
**Download All** or **Download ZIP** has handed it to your browser. For that same time, the line at
the foot of the card reads "Keep this tab open and in front. Closing it interrupts the download."
Chrome and Edge can unload a tab in the background to save memory, and that discards the files as
closing it would. For that same time the page does not reload by itself after Floe's web app is
updated: see [The page reloaded by itself](/troubleshooting#the-page-reloaded-by-itself). Once every
file has arrived and been saved, the line reads "End-to-end encrypted. Files are never stored on a
server."

While the two devices are connected, the page asks your browser to keep the screen on, where the
browser supports it. Browsers let go of that whenever the tab is hidden, and the page asks again
when you come back.

## The sender has to stay connected

Files stream from the sender's device, so if they close their tab, quit Floe Desktop, or stop the
[CLI](/glossary#cli) before the transfer finishes, the connection drops and nothing resumes it.

What you see when the sender leaves depends on how far things had got:

| When the sender left | What the page shows | What to do |
|----------------------|---------------------|------------|
| Before the two devices connected | The second step of the card changes to "The sender is not connected". | Keep the page open if they are coming back. A sender in the browser who returns starts the transfer by itself. |
| After they connected, before any file finished | A red banner, usually "The sender ended the transfer before it finished." Other wordings are under the table. | Reload the page to try again, or ask the sender for a new link. |
| After at least one file finished, with more still to come | An amber line under the list, such as "Received 1 of 3 files. The transfer ended before the rest arrived." | Save what arrived, and ask the sender to send the rest. |

In the second case the banner can also read "The sender ended the transfer. Ask them to start it
again." When the sender's side stopped with a reason, the banner shows that reason instead. A
sender in the browser gives one when it stops by itself or refuses a relayed path. See
[Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting#the-sender-ended-the-transfer).

In the third case the badge goes back to **Offline**, the progress row goes away, and the files
that arrived stay listed. See
[Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting#the-transfer-ended-before-the-rest-arrived). The rest needs a new
transfer: Floe [cannot resume](/how-it-works/known-limitations#no-resume) one that stopped.

A reload works while a sender in the browser still has their page open. Files that already arrived
stay in the list, and can still be saved, for as long as the page stays open.

This is what the web app at floe.one shows. A web app that someone hosts themselves can run an
older version, where a few of these lines read differently: see
[What an older web app shows](/self-hosting/operations#older-web-app-versions).

Your own connection to Floe's server can drop for a moment while the two devices are still
connecting. The page then joins again by itself. If the server still counts the page's old
connection, the page keeps trying for about a minute before it shows "Link Invalid". Whether that
saves the transfer depends on the sender:

| The sender is using | What happens |
|---------------------|--------------|
| A browser | Their page builds a fresh connection, and the transfer starts. |
| `floe send` or Floe Desktop | They treat it as you leaving, and their transfer ends. |

A device that comes back on a different network can be refused either way. See
[How a transfer works](/how-it-works/signaling#a-room-holds-exactly-two-people). Once the two
devices are connected, a drop in the server connection no longer matters, and the page does not
join again.

## Receiving in the terminal or the desktop app instead

Both write straight to disk as the bytes arrive and rebuild folder structure, which makes them the
better choice for anything multi-gigabyte. A browser can only take what that browser can hold, and
a phone holds less than a computer. Neither app runs on a phone: for a large transfer to one, see
[Known limitations](/how-it-works/known-limitations#a-browser-receiver-is-limited-by-what-the-browser-can-hold).

A [share link](/glossary#share-link) works in `floe receive` and in the Floe Desktop **Receive** tab.
A link from a [self-hosted](/glossary#self-hosted) Floe also needs that server's address: see
[Use a self-hosted server](/cli/self-hosted-server).

```bash Terminal
floe receive "https://www.floe.one/?s=8262e1b4#room=3f2b9c1e-7a4d-4c2e-9b1f-8e6d5c4b3a21"
```

If the sender gave you a [room code](/glossary#room-code) instead, three words, pass that. The
browser has no field for one.

```bash Terminal
floe receive olive-tiger-castle
```

See [floe receive](/cli/receive) and [Receive files in the desktop app](/desktop/receiving).

## If something goes wrong {#if-the-link-does-not-work}

- "Link Invalid": the link is incomplete, in use, or locked to other networks. See
  [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting#link-invalid).
- "The sender is not connected", or the card stays on "Waiting for the sender to start": see
  [The transfer never starts](/troubleshooting#the-transfer-never-starts).
- "Connection failed. Ask the sender to try again.": see
  [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting#could-not-connect-ask-the-sender-to-enable-network-relay-and-try-again).
